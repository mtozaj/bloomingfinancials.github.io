# Tax content review

Reviewed October 8, 2026 against IRS, California Franchise Tax Board (FTB), Employment Development Department (EDD), and Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) guidance. This review covers all seven educational tax articles and related homepage, service-page, and blog-index copy. It includes factual corrections, material eligibility qualifications, and wording that previously implied a guaranteed result.

## What changed and why

| Area | Correction or addition | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start-up and organizational costs | Explained the separate $5,000 allowances, the separate $50,000 phaseout thresholds, and 180-month amortization once the active business begins. Distinguished equipment, acquisition, capital-raising, and other separately governed costs. | Pre-opening spending is not automatically a current deduction. |
| Equipment deductions | Replaced outdated bonus-depreciation phase-down language with the restored 100% rule for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Added acquisition-date, business-use, vehicle, Section 179, and de minimis-election qualifications. | Purchase timing, eligibility, elections, and state differences can change the deduction. |
| Home office and health insurance | Qualified exclusive-use and principal-place-of-business rules, income limits, employee treatment, and California adjustments. Added the established-plan, owner-payroll, business-income, subsidized-employer-coverage, and long-term-care limitations for health insurance. | A home workspace or personally paid insurance premium does not automatically qualify. |
| Research expenses and credits | Clarified qualifying research, excluded activities, the federal payroll-credit election, and the separate California calculation. Distinguished the research credit from domestic research-expense deductions under Section 174A and foreign research amortization. | Ordinary software development and cloud spending do not automatically earn a credit, and deductions and credits are different benefits. |
| California LLC and corporation taxes | Explained that the broad first-year LLC $800 exemption applied to 2021 through 2023, while qualifying new corporations have a different minimum-tax exception. Corrected the LLC fee base and its $250,000 threshold. Explained that the general S-corp 1.5% tax is subject to an $800 minimum, rather than adding both amounts. | Readers could otherwise underestimate LLC obligations or double-count corporation tax. |
| Entity classification and owner pay | Clarified LLC tax elections, activity-dependent reporting, S-corp shareholder eligibility, reasonable compensation for services, retained pass-through income, and distribution/basis rules. Removed the implication that every shareholder needs payroll solely because of ownership. | Legal entity type, tax classification, compensation, and cash withdrawals are not interchangeable. |
| 2025 federal changes | Added the SALT phase-down and floor, Child Tax Credit SSN rules, and separate Schedule 1-A requirements. Corrected the blanket joint-filing rule: it applies to married claimants of the tips, overtime, and senior deductions, but not to the car-loan-interest deduction. | An eligible separate filer could otherwise be incorrectly excluded from the car-loan deduction. |
| Tips, overtime, seniors, and vehicles | Qualified reporting and income limits, FLSA-required overtime premiums, personal-use vehicle and financing requirements, and the distinction between the senior deduction and taxation of Social Security. Explained California nonconformity. | Federal deductions do not make all tips or overtime tax-free or automatically reduce California tax. |
| Trump Accounts | Qualified pilot eligibility, contribution limits and exclusions, and later distributions. | The pilot deposit is not automatic, and access beginning in the year the beneficiary turns 18 does not make distributions automatically tax-free. |
| Withholding and estimated taxes | Updated 2026 W-4 Step 4(b), DE 4 guidance, federal payment thresholds, prior-year AGI rules, and installment timing. Added California's lower balance-due threshold and loss of the prior-year safe harbor at current-year California AGI of at least $1 million, or $500,000 for separate filers. | A federal safe-harbor calculation does not settle California obligations, and avoiding a penalty can still leave a balance due. |
| Payroll | Separated employee PIT/SDI withholding from employer UI/ETT/FUTA, distinguished deposits from returns, and added lookback and $100,000 deposit rules. Clarified lawful pay frequency, worker classification, and the general federal 1099-NEC threshold of $600 for 2025 versus $2,000 for 2026. | A quarterly filing deadline is not necessarily the tax-deposit deadline; issuing a 1099 does not establish contractor status. |
| Family credits | Added CalEITC ITIN eligibility, the Young Child Tax Credit zero/negative-earned-income exception, Foster Youth Tax Credit criteria, nonrefundable California care/renter-credit limits, and education-credit restrictions. Qualified separated-parent and caregiver rules. | Similar-sounding credits have different eligibility, refundability, and dependency rules. |
| Marketplace health coverage | Added the 2026 return of the general 400%-of-poverty federal eligibility ceiling and removal of excess advance Premium Tax Credit repayment caps. Encouraged prompt Marketplace updates. | A household relying on the 2025 rules could face a substantially larger repayment for 2026. |
| RSUs and stock sales | Qualified the vesting examples for deferred-settlement plans, described 22% as an optional supplemental-wage withholding method, and distinguished withholding from final tax. Added the full 61-day wash-sale window, cross-account/IRA considerations, and California sourcing after a move. | These omissions can lead to underpayments, incorrect loss deductions, or overlooked California income. |
| Notice support and marketing | Requested every notice page through the secure portal, qualified transcript authorization and agency-controlled outcomes, and explained continuing payment-plan interest and penalties. Replaced blanket compliance and penalty-free language. | The site should explain the service accurately without promising an agency result or implying interest stops during a payment plan. |
